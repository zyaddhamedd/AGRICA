# AGRICA Hero Film — Locked Production Direction

## Status and purpose

This document locks the production direction for the five AGRICA homepage hero videos. It does **not** authorize final video generation.

The five films must work as one connected proof of a real Egyptian export operation:

1. **THE ORIGIN** — the land and the farmer
2. **THE HARVEST** — the people and the crop
3. **THE STANDARD** — the controls and the facility
4. **THE JOURNEY** — the operational handoff
5. **THE WORLD** — export readiness and onward movement

### Locked creative proposition

**One Egyptian citrus lot. Five proofs of AGRICA.**

Follow one believable citrus lot from orchard to export handover. The repeated crop, crate language, restrained AGRICA identity, and evolving time of day make the cards feel like chapters of one film rather than five agricultural stock clips.

The film should communicate a company that is:

- Egyptian in origin
- close to growers and workers
- disciplined in handling and quality control
- operational at commercial scale
- credible to an international procurement buyer

The buyer should infer capability from observable work. The film must not assert credibility through invented certificates, fake paperwork, decorative flags, maps, landmarks, or oversized logos.

## 1. Unified creative direction

### Cinematic identity

The visual language is **premium observational documentary with commercial finish**. It is composed and beautiful, but never immaculate to the point of disbelief.

- Natural Egyptian locations and plausible working environments
- Real people engaged in a task, not performing for camera
- Honest surface detail: dust in the orchard, scuffed field crates, normal fruit variation, brushed steel, corrugated board, pallet wrap and workwear texture
- Controlled, low-contrast color with natural greens, citrus orange, warm sand, AGRICA green and neutral industrial steel
- Restrained contrast and highlight roll-off; no neon greens, teal-and-orange blockbuster grade, artificial bloom or plastic skin
- Real-time operational motion; no speed ramps, hyperlapse, floating product shots or dramatic slow motion
- One motivated camera move per clip

### Narrative continuity system

Continuity comes from the lot and the workflow, not from forcing one face into every chapter.

Use these recurring anchors across all five chapters:

- the same crop: Egyptian oranges or mandarins of believable size and natural color variation
- the same field-crate family: off-white/grey vented reusable crates, visibly used but clean
- the same carton system: one approved AGRICA export carton design
- the same restrained uniform palette: olive, stone, sand and dark AGRICA green
- one consistent clean grade and camera package
- a visual handoff at the end of every clip that anticipates the next chapter

### Time-of-day arc

The sequence should feel like one working day:

| Chapter | Light | Narrative effect |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | soft dawn / early morning | heritage, place, beginning |
| Harvest | clean morning sun | activity, freshness, human skill |
| Standard | neutral controlled daylight | precision, hygiene, discipline |
| Journey | warmer late-afternoon light | momentum and handoff |
| World | restrained blue-gold transition | completion, scale, onward confidence |

### Camera and lens grammar

- Natural perspective as the default: approximately 35–50 mm full-frame equivalent
- Approximately 28–35 mm only for facility-scale views; avoid distorted ultra-wide shots
- Camera height should usually feel human: chest, shoulder or working-height, not constant aerial spectacle
- Allowed moves: locked frame with natural activity, slow push, short lateral track, restrained follow, or measured crane reveal
- Avoid crash zooms, whip pans, orbits, FPV flight, fake handheld shake and multi-axis showreel movement
- Movement begins gently, remains legible, and settles before the end frame

### Human direction

People are evidence of an operating company, not lifestyle models.

- Cast working-age Egyptian adults appropriate to the role
- Use natural task focus, brief colleague interaction, and ordinary body mechanics
- No one looks into camera, presents fruit to camera, smiles on cue, or adopts a catalogue pose
- Hands must perform a real action: checking, clipping, lifting, sorting, sealing, wrapping, scanning, driving or guiding
- Faces may be visible, but work remains the subject
- Show women and men where operationally credible; wardrobe and PPE must be appropriate to the task
- Preserve natural skin texture and ordinary variation; no beauty-ad retouching

### Hero-card framing lock

The live site displays an active card at roughly **16:9 on desktop** and approximately **4:3 on mobile**, while inactive cards become extremely narrow. Therefore:

- Produce a 16:9 master, but protect the central 60% of frame width as the 4:3/mobile action-safe area
- Keep the key face, hands, fruit interaction, machine action or pallet within the central 50–55%
- Keep verified AGRICA marks within the central 45%; never place the only brand cue near an edge
- Treat the outer 20% on each side as expendable composition, not critical story area
- Keep horizons level and faces away from top/bottom crop boundaries
- Design one instantly readable silhouette per chapter for poster and inactive-card recognition
- Make the opening frame useful as a poster; do not begin on blur, black, a transition or an empty establishing shot

### Duration and loop lock

- **Final editorial duration: 7.0 seconds per clip**, matching the current hero’s seven-second advance rhythm
- Generate or capture 8–10 seconds where possible, then select the strongest seven-second window
- Reserve the final 0.5–0.8 seconds for a settled operational image, not a freeze frame
- Use a gentle 6–10 frame visual/audio-neutral dissolve or a matched motion boundary only if a direct loop visibly jumps
- The films must communicate fully when muted; do not depend on dialogue, narration or sound cues
- No text overlays, captions, titles or baked-in interface graphics

## 2. Video-by-video directing plan

## 01 — THE ORIGIN

**Objective**  
Establish unmistakable Egyptian agricultural origin and human legitimacy before showing scale or machinery.

**Message**  
AGRICA begins with real Egyptian land and a real relationship to cultivation.

**Subject**  
An experienced Egyptian farmer in a working citrus orchard, wearing an authentic neutral-toned galabeya. He examines a branch or fruit with practiced, economical movement. The moment must feel observed, not directed.

**Human presence**  
One farmer is the clear symbol. Show his face naturally in three-quarter profile or profile, but make the relationship between person, tree and land the subject. Weathered hands and credible age/detail are positive. No fashion-model grooming.

**AGRICA branding presence**  
Keep this the lightest-branded chapter. Use one secondary operational cue only: an AGRICA field crate, a small cloth tag on a crate stack, or a nearby AGRICA field-team worker held softly in the background. Do not put a corporate polo on the farmer or brand the galabeya.

**Environment**  
A mature Egyptian citrus orchard with dense rows, dry earth, modest irrigation evidence and an open Nile Valley agricultural horizon. Avoid desert dunes, pyramids, tourist imagery and fantasy-lush vegetation.

**Composition**  
Farmer and fruit-bearing branch overlap in the center-safe zone. Include enough ground/horizon to establish place. The frame should still read as “farmer in orchard” when reduced to a narrow card.

**Camera movement**  
A slow 40–60 cm lateral track or gentle push through foreground leaves, ending on the farmer’s hand checking one fruit.

**Motion style**  
Leaves respond lightly to real wind; the farmer makes one complete action. No excessive leaf movement or artificial rack-focus pulsing.

**Lighting**  
Soft Egyptian dawn with warm low-angle light, controlled contrast and no theatrical sun flare.

**Duration recommendation**  
7.0 seconds. Action should become legible within the first second.

**Last-frame goal**  
A settled hand-to-fruit image with a field crate softly visible below or behind, preparing the cut into Harvest.

**Avoid**

- staged portraiture or eye contact
- generic European orchard cues
- costume-like, spotless or ornate galabeya
- camels, pyramids, flags or other shorthand clichés
- pristine identical fruit or over-lush fantasy trees
- prominent logos competing with the farmer

## 02 — THE HARVEST

**Objective**  
Show skilled team activity and connect origin to a real, handled crop.

**Message**  
AGRICA works with a real harvest team and moves produce carefully from tree to crate.

**Subject**  
Two or three workers harvesting citrus by hand into field bags or vented crates. The hero action is one clean pick-and-place cycle.

**Human presence**  
Workers remain focused on the task. One can be nearest camera while the others create depth. Clothing is practical and slightly varied; matching wardrobe should not make the team look cast.

**AGRICA branding presence**  
One or two muted olive/stone AGRICA T-shirts or lightweight work shirts with a small left-chest mark. A crate may carry one controlled AGRICA mark. Branded workers should mix naturally with unbranded practical layers.

**Environment**  
The same orchard family as Origin, with believable crate placement and a clear row for movement. Preserve natural fruit and leaf variation.

**Composition**  
Keep the picking hand, fruit and crate relationship centered. Use foreground citrus to provide depth without obscuring hands or faces. A crate stack can create the right-side structural line, provided it stays within crop safety.

**Camera movement**  
Short shoulder-height follow or steady lateral track along the orchard row.

**Motion style**  
Real-time hand harvest, light body movement, normal weight transfer and fruit settling naturally in the crate. Do not throw fruit.

**Lighting**  
Clean morning daylight, slightly warmer than neutral, with open shade on faces and controlled leaf highlights.

**Duration recommendation**  
7.0 seconds.

**Last-frame goal**  
The picked orange settles into a partially filled center-frame crate. The circular fruit motion prepares the transition to rolling fruit in Standard.

**Avoid**

- a single posed “happy worker” presenting fruit
- impossible hand anatomy or fruit appearing/disappearing
- unsafe ladders, overloaded bags or implausible picking mechanics
- glossy spotless uniforms
- slow-motion fruit drops
- excessive AGRICA marks on every person and crate

## 03 — THE STANDARD

**Objective**  
Provide the strongest proof of export-grade control, facility scale and disciplined handling.

**Message**  
AGRICA converts harvested produce into a controlled, inspected and packed export lot.

**Subject**  
A functioning citrus sorting/inspection line with one visible inspection action. The buyer should see both throughput and human control: rolling fruit, stainless/food-safe machinery, and an inspector removing or checking one piece.

**Human presence**  
Two to four packhouse workers or inspectors. Use appropriate hair covering, gloves and practical workwear. Staff must face the line or each other, never the camera.

**AGRICA branding presence**  
Small embroidered or printed AGRICA chest marks on selected uniforms; one approved mark on the near carton or line-side process board. Keep machine maker logos absent unless the real machine and usage rights are confirmed. Do not invent certifications.

**Environment**  
A clean but working packhouse: coherent machinery, believable conveyor direction, drainage/food-safe surfaces, realistic overhead structure and no unexplained luxury-factory polish.

**Composition**  
Use conveyor lines as depth guides into the center. Keep the inspection hand/action and a branded worker or carton within the central safe zone. The active-card poster should read immediately as “industrial quality control,” not merely “a pile of oranges.”

**Camera movement**  
Slow parallel dolly beside the line or measured push along the conveyor. One axis only.

**Motion style**  
Fruit moves at a believable shared belt speed, stays in physical contact with rollers, and does not duplicate or teleport. Worker cadence should match line speed.

**Lighting**  
Neutral, clean industrial daylight around 4300–5000 K with restrained highlights on steel. Preserve some ambient falloff; do not make the facility clinically white.

**Duration recommendation**  
7.0 seconds.

**Last-frame goal**  
A checked fruit passes into an open approved AGRICA carton, or a worker closes a filled carton in center frame, preparing the pallet/logistics chapter.

**Avoid**

- generic sci-fi automation or impossible machinery
- fruit moving in conflicting directions
- dirty food-contact surfaces or missing role-appropriate hygiene
- fake seals, certificates, QR codes, lot numbers or regulatory claims
- brand marks that warp, flicker or change spelling
- all workers wearing identical costume-like branded outfits

## 04 — THE JOURNEY

**Objective**  
Show that AGRICA can move a finished lot through a credible export handoff.

**Message**  
AGRICA coordinates the physical chain between packing and shipment.

**Subject**  
A wrapped pallet of approved AGRICA cartons moving by forklift through a loading bay toward a refrigerated container or reefer trailer. The action is the controlled handoff, not transport spectacle.

**Human presence**  
A forklift operator and one logistics team member guiding from a safe position. Faces are secondary; correct posture, sightlines and safe separation matter more.

**AGRICA branding presence**  
The approved AGRICA carton system is the primary brand surface. Add one small uniform chest mark or jacket mark on the visible logistics worker. The forklift, container and high-visibility vest should not become billboards.

**Environment**  
Real loading bay geometry with plausible dock plate, pallet wrap, carton stacking, forklift clearance and reefer/container threshold. Keep the area orderly but active.

**Composition**  
Pallet and container opening align on the central axis. Show enough forklift and worker context to explain the action without letting either edge carry critical information.

**Camera movement**  
Measured backward track ahead of the pallet or a locked three-quarter view with the pallet entering frame and settling centrally.

**Motion style**  
Slow industrial movement with visible weight and suspension. Wheels rotate correctly; pallet, forks and cartons remain rigidly related. No drifting or frictionless glide.

**Lighting**  
Warm late-afternoon exterior light meeting cooler loading-bay ambience, with believable exposure transition.

**Duration recommendation**  
7.0 seconds.

**Last-frame goal**  
The pallet settles just inside the container threshold, AGRICA carton face readable and centered. This becomes the opening visual logic for World.

**Avoid**

- unsafe staff placement near forks or between pallet and container
- mismatched pallet dimensions, floating wrap or collapsing cartons
- non-reefer container details when cold-chain handling is implied
- prominent third-party carrier branding without permission
- racing forklift, dramatic sparks, dust clouds or action-film camera motion

## 05 — THE WORLD

**Objective**  
Complete the story with calm export readiness and international scale.

**Message**  
The Egyptian lot is professionally prepared, secured and moving into global trade.

**Subject**  
A completed loaded reefer/container handover: the last pallet is aligned, doors are secured or the loaded vehicle begins a controlled departure from the export facility. Use one decisive image rather than a montage of ports and countries.

**Human presence**  
One or two loading/export team members support the action at medium or long distance. They perform a final check, secure a door, or step clear. Faces do not dominate.

**AGRICA branding presence**  
Visible approved carton faces through the doorway and one restrained AGRICA operational mark on a team jacket, seal tag or facility touchpoint. If a container mark is used, it must be small and plausible—not a full-container advertisement. Never fabricate customs marks, destination labels or certification badges.

**Environment**  
An Egyptian export facility or credible logistics yard with a reefer unit, clean hardstanding and restrained hints of onward infrastructure. Avoid generic skyline composites.

**Composition**  
The container or vehicle forms the central geometric anchor. Keep the loaded cargo or closed-door/seal action within the mobile-safe zone. Negative space may imply scale, but the card must not become visually empty.

**Camera movement**  
Very slow pullback or restrained static frame as the vehicle moves forward. A small controlled crane rise is acceptable only if it remains documentary and center-safe.

**Motion style**  
Calm, heavy, purposeful movement. End with a visually stable export-complete image. Do not make “the world” literal with globes, maps or flags.

**Lighting**  
Clean blue-gold transition light, grounded and realistic. No giant sunset, excessive flare or dramatic storm sky.

**Duration recommendation**  
7.0 seconds.

**Last-frame goal**  
A centered, secured refrigerated shipment moving away with the facility still legible behind it. The motion should be gentle enough to loop through a short dissolve back to the opening handover view.

**Avoid**

- airplanes, container ships, ports and world landmarks compressed into one clip
- fake destination names, customs seals or documents
- workers waving goodbye or posing
- an oversized AGRICA logo on the container
- impossible vehicle physics or unreadable changing container geometry
- a triumphalist advertising finish; the emotion is quiet confidence

## 3. Brand realism strategy

### Brand rule: one primary proof, one secondary echo

Every chapter should contain no more than one primary AGRICA brand surface and one quieter secondary echo. Across the full sequence, the repeated system creates recognition. Repeating large marks inside one shot creates distrust.

| Touchpoint | Locked treatment | Realism rule |
|---|---|---|
| Field T-shirt | muted olive, stone or sand; small left-chest AGRICA mark | cotton texture, normal wear, not every worker branded |
| Packhouse polo/jacket | dark green or neutral workwear; small embroidery/patch | paired with role-appropriate gloves/hair covering |
| Logistics layer | practical jacket or polo; small chest/upper-arm mark | high-visibility PPE remains functional, not promotional |
| Field crate | small one-color stencil or durable label | used consistently; no giant centered logo |
| Export carton | one approved, repeatable carton system | exact official logo; limited verified text; consistent dieline |
| Facility | one modest sign/process touchpoint only if spatially plausible | avoid freshly pasted logos on every wall or machine |
| Paperwork/seals | use only real, approved data | never let generation invent compliance claims |

### Uniform system

Create a small wardrobe bible before generation:

- **Field team:** stone, faded olive, muted navy, practical layers; one small AGRICA chest mark on selected people
- **Packhouse team:** dark AGRICA green/stone polo or light jacket; white/neutral role-appropriate PPE; clean but visibly functional
- **Logistics team:** dark work jacket or polo, neutral trousers, standard safety gear where required
- Keep logo scale, color, placement and garment tone identical whenever the same item recurs
- Avoid crisp matching outfits, oversized back prints, brand-colored gloves and fully coordinated caps; those read as costumes

### Packaging system

Approve one carton design before animation. It should include only:

- the exact AGRICA logo lockup
- one controlled produce identifier if verified
- simple handling graphics if verified and required
- real legal/operational copy only when supplied and approved

Do not ask a video model to invent carton typography. The preferred order is:

1. build or approve a clean carton keyframe with the official AGRICA art;
2. animate from that controlled frame;
3. inspect every frame for drift;
4. replace or motion-track the logo/carton face in post if the mark deforms.

### Workflow credibility

Every visible action must have an operational cause and effect:

- picked fruit goes into a crate
- crates move toward intake
- fruit follows one conveyor direction
- inspection removes/checks a specific piece
- packed cartons stack with consistent orientation
- pallet wrap contains the stack
- forklift tines stay under the pallet
- staff maintain safe positions
- pallet enters a geometrically plausible container
- the shipment is secured before movement

This chain is more persuasive than any slogan.

### What must not be fabricated

- certifications, accreditation logos or quality marks
- customs stamps, phytosanitary documents, seal numbers or destination labels
- ownership of a facility, vehicle, machine or farm not verified by AGRICA
- third-party carrier relationships
- worker identity or a claim that a generated person is an actual AGRICA employee

Until real facilities/people are supplied and cleared, these clips should be treated as **brand films representing AGRICA’s operating model**, not documentary evidence of a named site or employee.

## 5. Higgsfield production strategy

### Use a controlled still-to-motion pipeline

Do not attempt to obtain final clips from five unrelated text-to-video prompts. Build a repeatable visual system first.

1. **Prepare a visual bible**  
   Lock the AGRICA logo file, carton dieline, uniform palette, crate type, crop appearance, grade, lens family and light arc.

2. **Create chapter keyframes**  
   Produce one approved 16:9 start frame and one optional end frame for each chapter. Apply the desktop/mobile crop masks before approval.

3. **Lock consistency before motion**  
   Review the five stills side by side. Reject inconsistent fruit, facilities, wardrobe, logo scale, color grade or photographic style before spending video generations.

4. **Animate from approved frames**  
   Use image-to-video or start/end-frame control. Keep each prompt to one physical action and one camera move. Higgsfield’s current workflow supports [visual references and start/end-frame control](https://higgsfield.ai/ai-video) plus [explicit camera/lens control](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/ai-video-camera-control); those controls should be used to reduce variance rather than to add spectacle.

5. **Use motion reference selectively**  
   For harvesting, conveyor physics and forklift movement, use legally cleared reference footage when available. Higgsfield currently supports [motion reference](https://higgsfield.ai/ai/video/motion) and [video-to-video transformation that preserves source motion and composition](https://higgsfield.ai/video-to-video); this route is preferable to invented complex mechanics when source footage accurately represents the task. The supplied social screenshots are visual references, not automatically cleared motion assets.

6. **Protect the brand in compositing**  
   Use the official logo in the approved still where possible. If the moving generation distorts it, generate a clean surface and motion-track the real artwork in post. Never accept an approximate logo because it appeared “close enough.”

7. **Generate alternatives by shot, not by concept**  
   Create 3–5 candidates for each locked shot with the same framing and action. Select for anatomy, physics, logo stability and operational credibility—not for the most dramatic camera move.

8. **Unify in finishing**  
   Match exposure, white balance, contrast, grain, sharpening and motion cadence across all five. Remove UI remnants, watermarks, model artifacts and any unintended readable text.

9. **Make the web masters**  
   Finish clean 7.0-second muted masters, select dedicated poster frames, then make lightweight web encodes. Retain a high-quality archival master for future crops.

### Model/tool selection principles

- Prefer the highest-reliability photoreal image-to-video route available in the Higgsfield workspace at production time
- Use Cinema Studio controls to lock camera style, lens logic and restrained movement where available
- Use start/end frames when the last-frame handoff is compositionally important
- Use motion control/video-to-video only with cleared, physically credible source motion
- Keep seeds/settings recorded, but do not treat a seed as a substitute for visual QA
- Do not use automatic multi-shot storytelling inside a seven-second chapter; one shot per card is clearer and more stable
- Native sound is unnecessary for this muted hero implementation

### Continuity record for every generation

Log the following with each accepted candidate:

- chapter and shot version
- model/version and generation date
- input start/end frames and reference assets
- seed and all camera/lens settings where exposed
- exact prompt and negative constraints
- approved crop screenshots at desktop and mobile card ratios
- logo verification result
- anatomy/physics QA result
- rights/clearance status of any source footage

### Production gate order

Approve in this order; later polish must not rescue an earlier failure:

1. operational truth
2. human/anatomical truth
3. brand accuracy
4. crop safety and poster readability
5. cross-film continuity
6. cinematic polish

## 6. Prompting framework

### Prompt architecture

Use the same ordered structure for every chapter:

1. **Chapter and narrative function** — what this shot proves
2. **Location and time** — specific Egyptian environment and light
3. **Primary subject** — person/process/object and wardrobe
4. **Single action** — one complete, physically plausible action
5. **AGRICA brand surface** — exact approved placement and restraint
6. **Composition** — central action-safe zone and expendable side detail
7. **Camera** — height, lens family, one movement, speed and endpoint
8. **Lighting and grade** — natural exposure, clean low-contrast finish
9. **Motion behavior** — real-time weight, contact, wind and machinery rules
10. **Last-frame instruction** — stable handoff to the next chapter
11. **Negative constraints** — artifacts, clichés, overbranding and unsafe process

### Required realism language

Include language equivalent to:

> Ultra-realistic observational documentary-commercial footage of a real Egyptian produce operation; natural human anatomy and task behavior; physically correct tools, machinery, fruit contact, weight and motion; ordinary material wear and crop variation; restrained clean color grade; credible working environment; no staged advertising performance.

### Required branding language

Include language equivalent to:

> Use only the supplied official AGRICA identity. One subtle, correctly proportioned AGRICA mark on the specified operational surface; consistent placement and spelling across every frame; no invented brand text, no duplicated marks, no promotional signage. If exact mark stability cannot be maintained, leave a clean trackable surface for post-production branding.

### Human-presence constraints

Include language equivalent to:

> Egyptian adults appropriate to their operational roles, focused on work and unaware of camera; natural faces, hands, posture and clothing fit; no eye contact, presenting, posing or synchronized cast behavior; role-appropriate PPE and safe working distance.

### Composition constraints

Include language equivalent to:

> Compose for a 16:9 master with all essential action, face, hands and brand proof inside the central 60% width for a protected 4:3 mobile crop; strong readable opening/poster frame; no critical detail at left or right edges; level horizon; no baked-in text.

### Core negative block

Use and tailor this block for each chapter:

> No social-media UI, captions, subtitles, titles, watermarks or interface elements. No fake certificates, flags, maps, landmarks, QR codes, customs text or destination labels. No malformed hands, extra fingers, fused tools, duplicated people or fruit, changing faces, rubbery movement, floating objects, conveyor teleportation, warped cartons, logo drift or misspelled branding. No glossy CGI, plastic produce, beauty retouching, fantasy foliage, excessive lens flare, orange-and-teal grade, speed ramps, dramatic slow motion, drone spectacle, whip pan, orbit, crash zoom or shaky camera. No unsafe workplace behavior.

### Chapter prompt variables

| Origin | farmer checks one fruit/branch | subtle AGRICA field crate | slow lateral track/push | hand and fruit over crate |
| Harvest | worker picks and places one orange | small chest mark + crate mark | short steady follow | fruit settles in crate |
| Standard | inspector checks fruit as line advances | uniform mark + approved carton | parallel dolly/push | checked fruit enters carton |
| Journey | forklift advances one wrapped pallet | approved AGRICA cartons | measured backward track/locked three-quarter | pallet settles at container threshold |
| World | team completes handoff and shipment departs | carton faces + one operational mark | slow pullback/static departure | centered secured shipment moving onward |

## 7. Acceptance checklist

### Automatic rejection gates

Reject the clip immediately if any of the following is true:

- the chapter cannot be identified from a muted thumbnail
- Origin lacks a credible Egyptian farmer in a galabeya
- a required action is physically or operationally implausible
- hands, faces, fruit, tools, machinery, pallets, wheels or cartons visibly deform
- AGRICA is misspelled, warped, inconsistent or replaced by invented branding
- fake certification, customs, destination or compliance information appears
- a key subject or brand proof fails either desktop or mobile crop
- people pose, address the camera or feel cast for an advertisement
- the facility/process implies unsafe or unhygienic behavior
- social UI, subtitles, titles, watermarks or unwanted readable text remains
- the look is glossy CGI, hyper-saturated, cliché-heavy or obviously AI-generated
- the opening frame is unreadable or the loop contains a hard visual jump

### Per-clip approval scorecard

Score each category from 0 to 2:

- **Narrative proof:** the intended chapter/message is immediately clear
- **Operational truth:** tools, workflow, safety and cause/effect are credible
- **Human truth:** anatomy, wardrobe, behavior and role feel documentary-real
- **Brand truth:** the official mark is subtle, exact, stable and plausibly placed
- **Environment truth:** Egypt/farm/facility/logistics context is specific and believable
- **Motion truth:** object weight, contact, belt/vehicle direction and camera physics hold
- **Crop performance:** desktop, 4:3 mobile and narrow-card views remain readable
- **Continuity:** crop, crates, carton system, grade and timeline match the other chapters
- **Finish:** lighting, exposure, color, texture and compression feel premium but natural
- **Loop/poster:** the opening/poster reads instantly and the end settles cleanly

**Approval threshold:** all automatic gates pass, no category scores 0, and the clip scores at least **17/20**.

### Sequence-level approval

Approve the five-film set only when:

- it plays as one lot moving forward, not five isolated clips
- the brand becomes progressively more visible from field to export without ever becoming loud
- Egyptian origin is unmistakable in the first chapter and remains culturally respectful
- human presence is consistent with real work at every relevant stage
- the same crop, crate, carton and uniform systems remain coherent
- the light arc progresses naturally from morning origin to export completion
- no chapter duplicates another chapter’s narrative job
- the five opening/poster frames are distinct yet visibly part of one film
- all five clips survive the actual website’s desktop and mobile card crops
- a procurement buyer would read real capability before cinematic style

## Final production lock

The approved direction is not “five beautiful agricultural videos.” It is a compact, evidence-led brand film told across five hero cards:

**a real Egyptian farmer, a real harvest team, a controlled packhouse, a credible logistics handoff, and a professionally completed export movement.**

AGRICA branding should accumulate through the workflow—from a discreet crate and work shirt to a controlled carton system—so the final impression is of a company that exists inside the operation, not a logo applied on top of it.
