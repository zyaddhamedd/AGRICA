# AGRICA Product Card Data Audit

Audit date: 2026-09-27  
Scope: Produce catalogue only (`Fresh`, `Frozen`, `Dried`)  
Phase: Research and content architecture; no public product data or imagery implemented

## A. Executive audit

The canonical repository catalogue contains **37 products: 20 Fresh, 11 Frozen, and 6 Dried**. Product identity is centralized and stable, but buyer-facing trade data is not production-ready.

- Only 8/37 products have a populated `VERIFIED_PRODUCT_SPECS` entry. The name “verified” is not supported by a source registry or company approval record.
- Six entries publish `Class I / GlobalG.A.P.` and one publishes `Class I / Phytosanitary Certified`. These are unsafe as AGRICA/product claims without lot-, farm-, and programme-specific evidence.
- The other 29 card backs normally show only `Origin: Egypt` and a generic `Condition` row. That is too sparse to help a B2B buyer.
- `Origin: Egypt` is injected into every item, including frozen and dried lines, even though crop origin, processing country, and AGRICA supply origin are different facts.
- The current card renderer imposes a five-row priority list designed around fresh produce. It is unsuitable for IQF and dried products and can silently discard useful rows such as variety and condition.
- Product names and family labels exist in all five locales (English, Arabic, Russian, German, French). Technical values are only partially localized by string substitution; evidence, verification, and product-specific buyer copy are not localized or modeled.
- The seasonal subsystem is better structured than the card specs because it stores sources and confidence, but its `agricaApproved` flags have no approval provenance in the repository. It also mixes harvest, supply, and cold-stored windows.

**Decision:** do not publish the proposed specifications yet. First remove or quarantine unsafe claims, obtain the prioritized AGRICA confirmations in section G, then implement the typed evidence model in section H.

### B2. Claim, content, and architecture defects

1. **Unsupported certification language.** `GlobalG.A.P.` is displayed on oranges, lemons, grapes, strawberries, and pomegranates. No certificate number, scope, farm/producer, product scope, validity period, or chain-of-custody evidence exists in the repository.
2. **Phytosanitary wording is category error.** “Phytosanitary Certified” on potatoes reads as an enduring product attribute. Phytosanitary certification is normally shipment/destination specific.
3. **Grade is being presented as achieved.** `Class I` can be a relevant UNECE commercial class, but the card does not say “typical export grade” or “available to buyer specification,” and no lot inspection establishes compliance.
4. **False precision in packing.** Exact carton, punnet, mesh, and jumbo weights are not tied to AGRICA offer sheets. They may be plausible trade formats but are not verified availability.
5. **Temperature mismatches.** Current lemon `+8 to +10°C` conflicts with UC Davis guidance of 12–14°C depending on cultivar and duration. Potato `+4 to +8°C` ignores end use: the cited postharvest guidance differentiates table, frying, and chipping temperatures. Onion “Ambient / Controlled Cool” is not an actionable specification.
6. **Season concepts are mixed.** Oranges are `Nov–May` in card specs, `Nov–Jun` in the season module, and the USDA report distinguishes harvest beginning late November, variety-dependent harvest extensions, and cold-stored export extensions. Grapes are `May–Aug` in card specs but the USDA report distinguishes harvest through November from the principal May–August export window.
7. **Hardcoded origin is overbroad.** `buildProductAtlasItems()` assigns Egypt to all 37 lines. Frozen and dried products require separate raw-material origin and processing/packing country fields.
8. **Unproven approval state.** Eighteen seasonal entries carry `agricaApproved: true`, but no approver, date, evidence, or change history is recorded. Blueberry and guava are correctly flagged as unapproved/low-confidence but remain publicly listed.
9. **Fresh-only schema.** `ExportSpecification` offers harvest window, Brix, acidity, seed status, and average weight but lacks IQF cut, preparation, blend composition, broken percentage, treatment, frozen temperature, dried style, moisture basis, allergens/additives, and processing origin.
10. **Renderer discards data.** When more than five rows exist, `buildProductSpecRows()` keeps only origin, harvest, grade, packaging, and temperature. Varieties, condition, and other meaningful facts can silently disappear.
11. **Weak source traceability.** Card specs have no source IDs, review date, confidence, or field-level verification state. Season data has those fields but uses a separate registry and cannot support other facts.
12. **Localization is incomplete.** Names/families and labels are localized in five languages, but technical values are hardcoded English fragments with token substitution. Product-specific copy and verification-sensitive wording have no localization contract.
13. **No product deep link.** Every card shares the collection route. Enquiries can identify the product by ID internally, but buyers cannot link directly to an individual specification state.
14. **Image coverage is incomplete.** Twelve products have mapped AVIF assets and grapes use a legacy PNG. Twenty-four cards use generic family CSS visuals. Unused grapefruit, frozen guava, and frozen spinach assets do not correspond to the canonical catalogue and should not be mistaken for catalogue evidence.
15. **Adjacent company claims need governance.** Repository copy includes “ready for year-round supply,” “AGRICA Egyptian agricultural export facility and logistics operations,” “every AGRICA shipment follows one controlled process,” “FULL SPECIFICATION CERTIFIED,” and “EXPORT CLEARED.” These need the same evidence/approval discipline as card claims.

## C. Recommended card information architecture

The visible back should contain **six rows by default and never more than seven**. `Origin` should mean the crop/raw-material origin only when verified; processed goods may additionally need `Processed in`.

### Fresh card template

1. `ORIGIN` — public only after AGRICA confirms the supplied origin.
2. `TYPICAL EGYPTIAN SEASON` — harvest/export window clearly named; never imply live stock.
3. `VARIETIES / TYPE` — only AGRICA-supplied varieties; otherwise “Variety by buyer programme.”
4. `SIZE / GRADE` — product-specific measurement convention; “buyer specification” where the exact offer is not confirmed.
5. `COMMON EXPORT FORMATS` — researched category language, not an AGRICA availability promise.
6. `STORAGE / TRANSIT` — temperature; add RH only where operationally useful.
7. Optional `HANDLING NOTE` — chilling, ethylene, ventilation, or maturity note only when buyer-relevant.

Do not show Brix, acidity, average weight, or shelf life by default. Add them only when the product and buyer programme make them decision-critical and a current specification supports them.

### Frozen card template

1. `RAW MATERIAL ORIGIN` — if verified.
2. `PRODUCT FORM` — whole, halves, chunks, arils, florets, blend, fries, etc.
3. `CUT / CALIBRATION` — buyer-specification dependent unless AGRICA supplies a fixed range.
4. `PREPARATION` — e.g. washed, trimmed, blanched; publish only against supplier specification.
5. `COMMON PACK FORMAT` — generic trade format; exact net weights only after confirmation.
6. `FROZEN STORAGE` — `-18°C or colder` is defensible for quick-frozen food under Codex.
7. Optional `BUYER SPEC` — broken percentage, blend recipe, fry cut/coating, additives, or microbiological requirements.

Do not put a fresh harvest window or “year-round availability” on the public back. Raw-material season may be retained internally.

### Dried card template

1. `RAW MATERIAL ORIGIN` — if verified.
2. `PRODUCT FORM` — whole, slices, halves, flakes, granules, powder, leaf, etc.
3. `CUT / PARTICLE SIZE` — contractual/buyer specification where applicable.
4. `TREATMENT / COMPOSITION` — sulphited/unsulphited, salted, oiled, additives; only when documented.
5. `COMMON PACK FORMAT` — moisture-barrier inner pack and outer format; exact weight after confirmation.
6. `STORAGE` — cool, dry, dark; protect from moisture and pests.
7. Optional `SPECIFICATION NOTE` — moisture, defects, microbiology, or shelf life only from the actual supplier specification.

### Confidence and publication rules

- `HIGH`: authoritative standard/government/university postharvest evidence directly supports the field.
- `MEDIUM`: official regional calendar or multiple credible sources agree, but variety/region varies.
- `LOW`: trade convention or supplier-dependent format; keep internal or phrase as buyer specification.
- A technical standard describes a product category; it does **not** prove AGRICA supplies a compliant lot.
- A field can be published only when `publicStatus = approved` and any AGRICA-specific value has approver/date/evidence.
